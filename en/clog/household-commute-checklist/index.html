Buying in Los Altos, Mountain View, or Palo Alto: How to Test Your Household Commute

Distance on a map is a starting point. Test office trips, household pickups, and transfers at relevant times to compare homes against the routines you actually expect to keep.

Alongside layout and natural light, ask a practical question when comparing homes: what would an ordinary workday look like if we lived here?

For households considering Los Altos, Mountain View, and Palo Alto, a commute test deserves its own place in the home search. Hybrid work can mean different office days, while pickups, errands, and evening activities may fall to different household members.

1. Start with arrival times

Write down the places you need to reach and when you need to be there. Use the actual office entrance, pickup location, or activity destination. Separate recurring trips from occasional ones, and note who handles each journey. This helps identify the routes that matter most to test.

2. Measure the complete journey

For driving, include getting out of the house, parking, and walking to the destination. For transit, include the walk to the station, waiting, transfers, and the final connection. Straight-line distance from a listing to a station cannot describe that entire experience.

If you expect to cycle or walk part of the way, try the relevant crossings and check where you would leave your bike. Consider the bags or other items you normally carry. A short distance on a map still needs to work for your routine.

3. Plan with official tools and check again before leaving

Caltrain’s official travel page provides a trip planner, schedules, real-time information, and a live train map. VTA offers trip planning and explains the different roles of service alerts and real-time information. Look up the date, direction, and arrival time you need, then check for relevant changes before starting your journey.
Caltrain: https://www.caltrain.com/go
VTA: https://www.vta.org/

4. Test at relevant times and record the conditions

Where practical, travel close to the hours you expect to keep after moving. Note the date, departure time, weather, travel mode, and any temporary construction or service changes. A trip after a weekend open house can offer useful observations; add a weekday test for a workday routine.

Each test is a snapshot. Recording how long that particular journey took and the conditions you encountered is more useful than treating it as a permanent commute promise attached to the property.

5. Check the return trip and a backup

A workable morning trip is only part of the day. How would you return after a late meeting? Who could handle a pickup if plans changed? Try a backup route if it is important to your household. Discuss the results alongside space, budget, and your other home requirements.

Keep the same comparison sheet for each candidate property: primary route, observation time, parking or transfer details, and questions still to test. The purpose is to make household tradeoffs easier to discuss.
