Moving into Los Altos, Mountain View, or Palo Alto: Your New-Home Water Checklist

Care continues after closing. Organize your water account, meter information, usage records, and irrigation schedule to make everyday home maintenance easier.

After getting the keys, most households are busy moving furniture, connecting internet service, and organizing school and work routines. Add one practical task to that list: understand your home’s water service, how to track usage, and whom to contact with questions.

This checklist is for families settling into Los Altos, Mountain View, or Palo Alto. Sellers can also use it to organize a helpful handover.

1. Confirm the water provider for the property

Start with handover documents and billing information. Verify the service address, account start date, and provider contact details. Do not assume the provider from the city name alone. Valley Water’s website links to a tool for finding your water retailer if you need help identifying it.

Save customer service and emergency reporting contacts where household members can find them. For a condominium or townhome, ask management which water-related matters belong to the resident and which should go through the association or property manager.

2. Make the handover information useful

Ask someone familiar with the property to explain the meter location and the home’s customer-side shutoff. Record the instructions. If equipment is unfamiliar or access is difficult, ask the provider or a qualified professional for help rather than forcing a cover or operating an unidentified valve.

Note the move-in date and any meter information you can safely read. If there is an irrigation controller, photograph the existing settings so you can discuss them later with the person maintaining the garden.

3. Understand usage before comparing bills

On the first bill, identify the billing period, consumption, and units. Note unusual activity such as moving-day cleaning, visitors, or landscape work. When comparing later bills, consider the length of the billing period and household routines before comparing the total charge.

Ask the provider about readings, billing questions, and available account tools if something is unclear. A neighbor’s bill is context, rather than a target your household must match.

4. Use official guidance for follow-up checks

Valley Water’s indoor water-use page offers guidance on meter checks, toilet leaks, and flow measurements, including links to Chinese-language demonstration videos. Follow the official instructions and seek professional help when you are unsure how to proceed.

Observations can help you ask better questions. A check that shows no obvious issue does not establish the condition of every pipe in the home. Persistent unusual readings, dripping, or dampness deserve further investigation by an appropriate professional.

5. Include the garden in the handover

Discuss irrigation zones, controller operation, and responsibility for future adjustments with the landscape maintenance provider. Keep equipment instructions, service contacts, and unresolved tasks in one folder. Sellers can prepare the same information to help the next household become familiar with the property.
